Trackify Operating Flow

One pass through the system as a person actually moves through it — signing in, landing in a company and branch, working across the seven modules, and signing out. Every status, header and permission named below is the one the code uses.

10 roles 38 staff screens 7 modules 1 driver app 8h session
1

Sign in

Email and password against /api/auth/login.

Credential
Password checked with bcrypt. Never stored or logged in plain text.
Returns
A signed JWT plus the account's roles, permissions and the company/branch pairs it may work in.
Lifetime
8 hours, from JWT_EXPIRES_IN.

The browser keeps this under ttms_auth. Every later request sends it as a bearer token — the server holds no session of its own.

2

Land in a company and branch

This is the step that makes Trackify multi-company rather than one database with a filter.

The first access pair from sign-in becomes the operating context, shown in the top bar as AstreaBlue Logistics · Davao Branch. Every API call then carries X-Company-Id and X-Branch-Id, and middleware re-checks them on each request before any controller runs:

Missing pair
400 — company and branch operating context are required.
Branch check
The branch must exist, be active, and belong to that company.
Access check
The user needs a live user_company_access row for the pair — within its effective_from / effective_to dates. Access can be dated to expire on its own.

A branch-scoped role sees only its branch. A company-scoped role may switch branches from the top bar, which re-resolves permissions for the new scope.

3

Permissions resolve for that scope

Enforced twice, and the two are not interchangeable.

In the screen
<Can permission="trip.approve"> hides what you cannot do, so the interface stays honest — convenience, not security.
On the route
requirePermission("trip.approve") refuses it regardless of what the screen showed. This is the real gate.

A role without a permission does not get a broken page — it gets a page that says it has no access, and the sidebar never offers the route.

4

The dashboard reads the day

Four counts, then the work that needs a decision.

Trips today, in transit, awaiting approval, open exceptions — then trip activity over seven days, the on-time rate, vehicles on the road, and the one run worth watching with its real milestones. A step with no actual timestamp is drawn hollow and labelled planned against its scheduled time, never as though it happened.

Where the numbers come from

Every figure is derived from records already fetched — trips, exceptions, vehicle stats. Nothing on the dashboard is a stored metric, so nothing can drift from the tables it summarises. Where a figure cannot be computed yet (an on-time rate with no closed trips) the panel shows an em dash rather than a zero.

5

Operations runs the trip

The spine of the system. Everything else hangs off a trip ticket.

create draft Dispatcher raises the ticket
submit for_validation Checked for completeness
validate for_approval Sent up for sign-off
approve approved Approver commits it
assign assigned Driver and vehicle paired
release released Cleared to depart
start in_transit Departure time stamped
deliver delivered Arrival time stamped
close operationally_closed Done, ready to bill

Any state before transit can be cancelled, and a rejected trip can be discarded the same way. A returned load closes through returned. Each move is one locked transaction that writes a status-history row — there is no way to skip a step, and no delete: a trip is cancelled, never removed.

Trips

/operations/trips

Every ticket and where it sits in the workflow, as cards or a table. Origin and destination are geocoded on save, so distance and drive time are real numbers.

Dispatch

/operations/dispatch

Approved-but-unassigned trips beside available drivers and vehicles, with the yard's real spare capacity in kilograms. Assignment validates the pairing before it commits.

Hands off to Live Tracking, once released.

Live Tracking

/operations/live-tracking

Active runs, the map, and a detail panel with load, route and GPS freshness. A unit that stops reporting recedes rather than raising an alarm.

Fed by the driver app's GPS pings.

Exceptions

/operations/exceptions

Delays, breakdowns and incidents raised against a trip, filtered by severity and resolved with a note.

6

The driver works from the phone

A separate app on its own credentials, deliberately outside the staff chain.

Drivers sign in with an employee number and a PIN and receive their own scoped token — they never hold a staff session, and the driver routes sit outside the staff authentication and context middleware entirely. App access is granted per driver by fleet staff and can be revoked without touching the driver's record.

  • See only trips currently assigned to them.
  • Share live location, which is what Live Tracking plots.
  • Start the trip, then confirm delivery.
  • Log fuel, tolls and other costs with a photo of the receipt from the camera.

A delivered trip stays visible for three days so receipts can still be filed after a late arrival. The expense endpoint enforces the same window.

7

Fleet and Warehouse keep the trip possible

Two modules that answer to Operations rather than lead it.

Fleet

vehicles · drivers · maintenance
availability · compliance

The vehicle registry with capacity, odometer and service interval; driver records and licences; maintenance jobs; who and what is free right now; and expiry alerts for registrations and licences.

Feeds Dispatch — a vehicle in maintenance cannot be assigned.

Warehouse

inventory · stock movements
transfers · cargo release · cargo return

Stock by location with reorder levels, every movement, branch-to-branch transfers, and the cargo handover itself.

Gates the trip — cargo releases only for a dispatched trip, and only once.

Stock is never adjusted by hand

Quantities move only through a movement, a transfer, or a maintenance job consuming its parts. Insufficient stock blocks the operation rather than going negative.

8

Finance turns the trip into money

A closed trip is the starting point for both what it cost and what it earns.

trip expense→ expense voucher→ approved→ paid
closed trip→ invoice line→ invoice→ journal entry→ BIR / EIS

Trip Expenses

/finance/trip-expenses

Fuel, tolls, parking, meals, lodging, repairs. Claims filed by drivers arrive as submitted and sit in a review queue with the receipt shown beside them; approving is what makes a claim recorded.

Control Only a recorded expense can reach a voucher — nothing a driver types posts to the books unreviewed.

Expense Vouchers

/finance/expense-vouchers

Recorded expenses grouped into one payable document, approved, then paid — which marks its expenses reimbursed.

Invoices

/finance/invoices

Billed to the customer, with each line referencing the trip it came from, so an invoice can always be traced back to the runs behind it.

Journal Entries

/finance/journal-entries

Double-entry lines against the chart of accounts. Debits and credits must balance before an entry can post.

BIR / EIS

/finance/bir-eis

The handoff to the client's own statutory reporting system. Trackify prepares and feeds the records; it is not the system of record for filing.

9

Master Data, Reports and Administration

The three supporting modules — one defines the vocabulary, one reads it back, one governs it.

Master Data

customers · suppliers · items
warehouses · accounts · tax codes

The shared lists every other module picks from. Change a customer here and every trip and invoice referring to it follows.

Reports

operations · fleet · expenses
financial · compliance

Date-ranged reads over the same tables the screens use — a report and the screen it came from can never disagree.

Administration

companies · branches · users
roles · integrations · settings · audit log

Companies and their branches, user accounts and the company/branch access that decides what each one may open, role templates built from the permission catalogue, and the audit log.

Written throughout Every consequential action records who did it, in which company and branch, and from what address.

↩

Sign out

Deliberately, or on its own.

By choice
Clears the stored session and the operating context — ttms_auth, ttms_company_id, ttms_branch_id.
On expiry
Any request answered 401 ends the session the same way, wherever in the app you were. No screen is left holding a dead token.

Because the token carries its own expiry, a session ends on its own after eight hours whether or not anyone signs out.

One rule underneath all of it. The screen decides what to show; the server decides what is allowed. Permissions, the company and branch context, the trip's next legal state, and whether an expense may reach a voucher are all settled on the server, so the same answer comes back whether a request arrives from the app, the driver's phone, or anything else.
